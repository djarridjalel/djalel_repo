What this is actually for

Regulated packaging is a systems problem before it is a visual one.

A carton has to satisfy a regulator, survive a print run, sit beside eleven of its own siblings on a pharmacy shelf and still be picked up by someone who is unwell and in a hurry. Most of that work happens before anything is drawn.

What a client is buying is a set of rules that keeps holding once the catalogue doubles and the designer is no longer in the room.

Packaging architecture

Rules a range can grow into

Variant logic, dosage hierarchy, indication clarity and the mandatory information that regulation requires — resolved once, at system level, so the thirty-first product does not need a new decision.

Brand identity

Marks that survive every substrate

A mark that appears on a screen, a printed carton and one ink on blister foil is really three marks. Drawing all of them up front is what stops a brand improvising them later.

Product-family coherence

Family resemblance, without uniformity

Shelf recognition depends on what stays constant; sales depend on what changes. The system has to make both legible at two metres and at arm's length.

Creative direction

From strategy to the print floor

Naming support, art direction, campaign, film and exhibition — carried through production rather than handed over at the artwork stage, which is where most systems quietly come apart.
