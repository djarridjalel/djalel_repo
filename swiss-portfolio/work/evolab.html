The brief

Evolab sells prescription and OTC medicines through pharmacies. The catalogue had grown faster than any system behind it. Products from the same manufacturer did not look related, and every launch restarted the design discussion.

Three audiences read the box: pharmacists, prescribing physicians and patients. One identity had to work for all of them.

Constraints
Mandatory informationINN name, strength and dosage form lead the hierarchy, ahead of the brand.
Two scriptsFrench and Arabic on every face: left-to-right and right-to-left on one carton.
Three substratesScreen, four-colour carton and one-ink blister foil. The mark works on all three.
Dosage variantsTwo strengths of the same molecule must never be confused at the counter.
Process
  1. AuditMapped every existing carton and where the hierarchy broke.
  2. RulesGrid, type scale and colour-by-strength logic, written as a one-page artwork guide.
  3. Pilot SKUOmeprotect first, reviewed with regulatory before rollout.
  4. RolloutFive SKUs from the same template, then the trade-show stand.
  5. Print controlProofs and press checks with the printer on each run.
Results

Relevant for your team

The same method applies to a Swiss range: audit the cartons, write the rules, pilot one SKU with regulatory, then roll out. Only the mandatory text changes, for example French, German and Italian and the Swissmedic requirements.
