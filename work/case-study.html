Braille: limit

The constraint

Regulation took the lower third of every front panel: composition, dosage, storage, batch, expiry and Braille, in three languages, at a minimum body size that could not be argued down.

What remained for the brand was a band roughly 40 mm deep, shared with a product name that ran to nine words on the longest SKU.

Sixty cartons had to be recognisable as one range from two metres, and correct from twenty centimetres.

The object

PRINTED CARTON — real photograph, not a renderFull bleed, 21:9. The single most important slot on this page. Raking light, real board, no mock-up template.

The printed carton. Photograph, not a render — board texture, foil, and the fold all have to be visible.

Braille: detail

The tension

The mandatory block is set at 7 pt. The product name is set at 28 pt. Nothing sits between them, and that gap is what makes a pharmaceutical carton look either controlled or cheap.

The decision was to stop fighting the block and give it a frame: a fixed measure, a fixed leading, a fixed distance from the fold, repeated on all sixty.

MACRO CROP — typography at real size1:1. Shot at 100%. The mandatory block and the product name in the same frame.
